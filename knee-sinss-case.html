The Knee That Didn’t Bounce Back

You are the PTA carrying out an established PT Plan of Care. Review the note, interview the patient, determine SINSS, then choose appropriate interventions.

PT Plan of Care Note

Patient: 42-year-old male, UPS delivery driver, recreational runner.

Chief Complaint: Right anterior knee pain x 3 weeks after increasing running mileage too quickly. Pain with stairs, squatting, running. Eases with rest and ice.

Relevant History: No prior knee injuries. No imaging. Occasional ibuprofen.

Objective Findings:
• Knee flexion: 130° (pain at end range)
• Knee extension: 0°
• Quad strength: 4/5 (pain)
• Hip abductors: 4-/5
• Tender patellar tendon, mild swelling
• Positive patellar compression test
• Pain descending stairs, squat pain at ~45°
• Running tolerance reduced to 0.5 miles

PT Clinical Impression: Patellar tendinopathy (reactive stage) with quad weakness and hip control deficits.

Short-Term Goals (2 weeks):
• Reduce stair pain by 50%
• Quad strength 4+/5
• Squat tolerance to 60°

Long-Term Goals (4–6 weeks):
• Return to running 2 miles pain-free
• Quad strength 5/5
• Full squat tolerance without pain

Treatment Plan: Isometric quad loading, hip strengthening, patellar tendon unloading strategies, activity modification, manual therapy PRN, progress to isotonic loading as tolerated.
